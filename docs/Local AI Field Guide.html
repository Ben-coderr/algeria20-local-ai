Chapter 1

The big picture

What is AI?

Normally, a programmer writes rules: “if the email contains the word lottery, mark it as spam”. This breaks quickly, because people change their words. In AI (more exactly, machine learning), nobody writes the rules. We show the computer a huge number of examples, and it finds the patterns by itself.

What is an LLM?

An LLM (Large Language Model) is an AI that learned from a huge amount of text. Its main skill is simple: given some text, it predicts the next word. It does this again and again, and the result looks like a conversation. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Llama and Qwen are all based on LLMs.

Everyday picture

ChatGPT is a car. The LLM is the engine inside it. In your session you take a free engine (Llama, Qwen) and build your own car around it.

What you will build

In your session you build an assistant that:

  • runs on your own laptop, with no internet and no API key;
  • reads your own documents and answers questions about them, with sources;
  • can use tools (search, calculator, notes) by itself;
  • is packaged so anyone can run it with one command.

This needs four skills, and they are the four demos of your session. Here is how all the pieces of the repo fit together. Come back to this drawing after each chapter: every chapter zooms into one box.

Why local and open-source?

Everyday picture

A cloud AI service (OpenAI, Google and others) is a restaurant: the food is excellent, but you pay for every meal, you often need an international card, and the restaurant sees your order. A local model is your own kitchen: you pay once for the equipment, cooking is free, and nobody sees what you cook.

Three real reasons matter for Algerian students and developers:

  1. Payment. Many cloud services need an international bank card. Local models need none.
  2. Cost. Cloud services charge for every question. Local models are free after the download (you only pay electricity).
  3. Privacy. Your documents never leave your computer. This matters for companies and for personal data.

The honest limits

  • Small local models are less capable than the biggest cloud models. For chat, summaries and document questions they are often good enough. For hard reasoning or very long documents, they can fail.
  • You need enough RAM and disk space (Chapter 4 shows how much).
  • You are responsible for updates, backups and security.
  • “Open-source” models differ in license. Some allow commercial use and some do not. Many are better called open-weights: you can download the model file, but the training data and code are not all public. Read the license before selling something built on a model.
Say it on stage

“Today you will see AI that runs without a card, without a bill, and without your data leaving the room.”

Chapter 2

Words you will hear

Keep this chapter open and come back to it. The words are grouped by the part of the session where you meet them first.

What a token looks like

Models do not read letters or whole words. They read tokens: small pieces of text. A sentence is cut like this (the exact cuts depend on the model):

The capital of Algeria is Algiers.

9 tokens for 7 words. In English, about 3 tokens make 2 words. Arabic and Darija usually need more tokens for the same sentence, which makes them a little slower and uses more of the context window.

Chapter 3

How an LLM works

It predicts the next token

Imagine your phone's keyboard suggesting the next word. An LLM is the same idea, trained on a gigantic amount of text and much more skilled. If you write “The capital of Algeria is”, the model gives a score to every possible next token. “Algiers” gets a very high score, so it is chosen. Then the model adds it to the text and predicts again. It repeats until the answer is done. This is why answers appear word by word on the screen, which is called streaming.

Training versus using

  • Training teaches the model from huge amounts of text. It takes weeks, special computers and a lot of money. The knowledge is frozen into the model file.
  • Inference (using the model) is what you do. It is much cheaper, and a normal laptop can do it with small models.

The model does not learn from your questions. After the answer, it forgets. Chat apps seem to remember because they send the whole conversation again every time:

Why it can be wrong

An LLM does not look facts up in a database. It continues text in the most likely way. Usually the most likely continuation is correct, but not always. When it does not know, it still produces something that sounds right. This is a hallucination. It is the number one problem in AI apps, and the reason RAG exists.

The two things a model knows

Temperature

Temperature controls how adventurous the model is when it picks the next token. Low temperature almost always takes the top choice: careful and repeatable (good for facts). High temperature spreads the chances out: more varied and creative (good for stories). For document questions, keep it low.

Model size

Bigger models (more parameters) are usually smarter but slower and need more memory. Chapter 4 has a drawing of how much RAM each model in your repo needs.

Common mistake

Saying “the model searched the internet” or “the model remembered me”. A plain model does neither. It only sees training plus the prompt.

Check yourself

Why do answers appear word by word?
The model produces one token per loop and the app shows each token as soon as it exists (streaming).
Does the model learn from your conversation? Why does a chat still seem to remember?
No, the model file never changes. The app sends the whole conversation again with every new message, so the model reads the history each time.
What are the only two sources of knowledge a model has?
What it learned in training (frozen, with a cutoff date) and what is in the prompt right now.
Which temperature would you use for questions about a handbook?
Low. You want the same careful answer every time.
Chapter 4

Running models on your own computer

What is Ollama?

Ollama is a free program that downloads, stores and runs LLMs on your computer. When you install it, it starts a small service in the background. Other programs, such as your Python scripts, talk to this service at http://localhost:11434. “Localhost” means “this computer”, so nothing goes over the internet.

Everyday picture

Ollama is a music player for AI models. The models are the songs on your disk. Ollama loads one and plays it when a program asks.

The commands you need

CommandWhat it does
ollama pull llama3.2:3bDownload a model (needs internet, only once)
ollama listShow the models you have
ollama run llama3.2:3bChat in the terminal. Type /bye to leave
ollama psShow which models are loaded in memory right now
ollama rm <name>Delete a model to free disk space

Two kinds of models

  • A chat model (llama3.2:3b, qwen2.5:7b) reads text and writes answers.
  • An embedding model (nomic-embed-text) does not chat. It turns text into numbers that capture meaning. You meet it in Chapter 7.

Will it fit on my laptop?

Rule of thumb: you need a little more free RAM than the size of the download. A model that is too big for your RAM becomes painfully slow.

Quantization: how a 7B model fits

Each of the 7 billion parameters is a number. Stored at full precision (16 bits each), a 7B model needs about 14 GB. Quantization stores each number with fewer bits (about 4), a bit like saving a photo as a smaller JPEG. You lose a little quality, and the model fits on a laptop.

Where is Hugging Face?

Hugging Face is a huge website where researchers and companies share models, datasets and demo apps. Think of it as GitHub for AI. You use it to discover models, read their descriptions and licenses, and share your own work. Ollama has its own library of ready-to-run models, which is why your demos download from there. Many of the same models exist on both.

Why the first answer is slow

At the first question, Ollama must load the model from disk into RAM (the “load once” arrow above). After that, answers are faster. Ollama also unloads a model after some minutes of silence. That is why your demo_commands.txt starts with a warm-up:

curl http://localhost:11434/api/generate -d '{"model":"llama3.2:3b","keep_alive":"60m"}'

This loads the model and asks Ollama to keep it in memory for 60 minutes. Run it for both models five minutes before you go on stage.

Reading the timing line

Your script prints something like first token 1.2s | total 9.0s | ~25 tokens/s.

Check yourself

What does “localhost” mean?
This same computer. A request to localhost never goes on the internet.
What is the difference between a chat model and an embedding model?
A chat model writes text. An embedding model turns text into a list of numbers that represents its meaning, used for search.
Why is the first question slower than the next ones?
Ollama first has to load the model file from disk into RAM. After that it stays loaded for a while.
Your laptop has 8 GB of RAM. Which chat model do you pick?
llama3.2:3b or llama3.2:1b. A 7B model would be slow or not fit next to the browser and other apps.

Virtual environment and pip

Every project needs libraries, and different projects need different versions. A virtual environment (.venv) is a private box of libraries for one project. pip installs libraries into it. requirements.txt lists what the project needs.

python -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate          # Windows: .venv\Scripts\activate
pip install -r requirements.txt
Common mistake

ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'chromadb' almost always means you forgot to activate .venv in this terminal window. Activate it, then run again.

Environment variables

Your project reads settings such as CHAT_MODEL from the environment, so you can change them without editing code:

CHAT_MODEL=qwen2.5:7b python 01_hello.py "Explain RAG"

On Windows PowerShell, write $env:CHAT_MODEL='qwen2.5:7b' on one line and the Python command on the next. The settings you can change this way are all in config.py: CHAT_MODEL, AGENT_MODEL, EMBED_MODEL, CHUNK_SIZE, CHUNK_OVERLAP, TOP_K, OLLAMA_HOST.

Git and GitHub

Git saves the history of your project, like “save game” points. GitHub is a website that stores Git projects online so others can see and download them.

Demo 1, line by line

Demo 1 has two files: config.py (settings) and 01_hello.py (the demo).

config.py

OLLAMA_HOST = os.getenv("OLLAMA_HOST", "http://localhost:11434")
CHAT_MODEL  = os.getenv("CHAT_MODEL", "llama3.2:3b")
client = Client(host=OLLAMA_HOST)

The first two lines read a setting from the environment, and use the value after the comma if nothing was set. The last line creates a client: a helper object that knows where Ollama lives and can send it messages. Every other file imports this same client.

01_hello.py

for chunk in client.chat(
    model=CHAT_MODEL,
    messages=[
        {"role": "system", "content": "You are a concise, friendly teacher."},
        {"role": "user",   "content": question},
    ],
    stream=True,
):
    print(chunk["message"]["content"], end="", flush=True)

Read it slowly:

  • client.chat(...) sends a conversation to the model.
  • messages is a list. Each message has a role: system sets the behavior, user is the person asking, assistant holds the model's earlier answers.
  • stream=True asks for the answer in small pieces as they are produced.
  • The for loop receives each piece (chunk) and prints it right away.

The script also measures time. It records when the first piece arrives (first_token), counts the pieces, and at the end divides the count by the total time. Each streamed piece is roughly one token, so the speed is labelled “~” (approximate). The total also includes the waiting time, so the real writing speed is a little higher than shown.

Try it

  1. Change the system message to “Answer in exactly 3 bullet points” and ask again.
  2. Run it with a second model (CHAT_MODEL=qwen2.5:7b) and compare the timing lines.
  3. Turn off Wi-Fi and run it again. It still works. This is the proof that everything runs on your machine.
Say it on stage

(Wi-Fi off) “I just disconnected from the internet. Let's ask again.” Then let the answer stream in silence for two seconds.

Check yourself

What does stream=True change?
The answer comes in small pieces as it is generated, instead of all at once at the end. The audience sees text immediately.
What is the difference between the system role and the user role?
The system message sets rules and personality for the whole conversation. The user message is the actual question.
Why do we print with flush=True?
Python sometimes waits and prints text in batches. flush=True forces each piece onto the screen immediately.
Chapter 7

RAG: chat with your documents

The problem

A model knows only its training and the prompt (Chapter 3). Ask it about your university rules, your company handbook or your course notes, and it has never seen them. It will invent an answer. We want it to answer from your documents instead.

The idea: an open-book exam

Everyday picture

In a closed-book exam you answer from memory, and you may guess wrongly. In an open-book exam you first find the right page in your notes, then write the answer from that page. RAG turns the model's closed-book exam into an open-book exam.

The name means Retrieval-Augmented Generation:

  1. Retrieve: find the paragraphs of your documents most related to the question.
  2. Augment: add those paragraphs to the prompt, next to the question.
  3. Generate: the model writes the answer, using only what it was given.

Part A: preparing the documents (done once)

  1. Load. rag_ingest.py reads every .pdf, .md and .txt file in data/. PDFs are read page by page, so each chunk remembers its page number.
  2. Chunk. Cut the text into pieces of about 800 characters, each overlapping the previous one by 150 characters (drawing below).
  3. Embed. Turn every chunk into an embedding with nomic-embed-text.
  4. Store. Save the chunks, their numbers and a label {source, page} in Chroma. That label is how the answer can later say [sahara_labs_handbook.md p.1].

Each time you run rag_ingest.py it deletes the old collection and starts fresh, so you never get duplicate chunks.

Why chunks overlap

def chunk(text, size=800, overlap=150):
    pieces, start = [], 0
    while start < len(text):
        pieces.append(text[start:start + size])
        start += size - overlap        # move forward, but step back a little
    return pieces

The real code also refuses an overlap bigger than the size (the loop would never move forward) and drops empty pieces.

What is an embedding, really?

Imagine a huge map of meaning. Texts about similar things are placed close together, and texts about different things are far apart. An embedding is simply the position of a text on this map, written as a list of numbers (hundreds of them, not just two). This is why RAG searches by meaning and not by exact words.

Part B: answering a question (every time)

  1. The question is turned into an embedding with the same model.
  2. Chroma returns the closest chunks (by default TOP_K = 4).
  3. The app builds the prompt: rules, chunks, question.
  4. The chat model writes the answer and mentions the source.
q_emb = client.embed(model=EMBED_MODEL, input=question)["embeddings"][0]
res   = collection().query(query_embeddings=[q_emb], n_results=k)

Line one turns the question into numbers. Line two asks Chroma for the k closest chunks. This is the actual prompt the model receives (from build_messages() in rag.py):

system · the rulesYou are a helpful assistant that answers ONLY from the provided context.
- If the answer is not in the context, say exactly: "I don't know based on these documents."
- Be short and clear. Mention the source like [source p.page].
- Answer in the same language as the question.
user · context (the retrieved chunks)Context:
[sahara_labs_handbook.md p.1]
## Leave
Full-time employees receive 24 days of paid annual leave per year. …

[sahara_labs_handbook.md p.1]
… (three more chunks)
user · the questionQuestion: How many days of leave do full-time employees get?

The most important line

The system prompt says: answer only from the context, and if the answer is not there, say “I don't know based on these documents.” This one rule turns a model that invents things into a trustworthy assistant.

Say it on stage

(after “Who is the CEO of Sahara Labs?”) “It said I don't know. That is not a bug. That is the most important feature of this app.”

The three settings that matter

SettingToo lowToo high

Things that go wrong

  • Scanned PDFs are pictures, not text. This code cannot read them (it would need OCR).
  • Tables and columns can be read in a mixed-up order.
  • Another language: nomic-embed-text works best in English. For Arabic or French documents, test a multilingual model such as EMBED_MODEL=bge-m3 and compare scores.
  • Changing the embedding model means you must run rag_ingest.py again, because numbers from two different models cannot be compared.
Common mistake

Saying “RAG trains the model on your documents”. It does not. Nothing in the model changes. RAG only chooses what the model reads for this one question.

Check yourself

What are the three words behind RAG, and what does each do?
Retrieve (find the related chunks), Augment (put them in the prompt), Generate (the model writes the answer from them).
Why do chunks overlap?
So a sentence or idea that falls on the border between two chunks still appears complete in at least one of them.
Why is “I don't know” a good answer in your demo?
The CEO is not in the handbook. Saying “I don't know” proves the app answers only from documents and does not invent.
Why must you index again if you change the embedding model?
Each embedding model makes its own map of meaning. A question embedded with model B cannot be compared with chunks embedded with model A.
How does the answer know which page it came from?
Each chunk is stored with a label {source, page}. build_messages() writes that label above each chunk in the prompt, and the system prompt asks the model to cite it.
Chapter 8

Evaluation: how to know it is good

The problem

You try three questions, they work, and you feel happy. Then you change the chunk size, and you feel it is “a bit better”, but you are not sure. Feelings are not enough. A real AI app needs a measurement.

The idea: a small exam for your app

Write a list of test questions with the answer you expect. Run them all, count how many are correct, and get a score. Now every change can be compared with a number.

eval_questions.json holds 10 questions. Eight have an answer in the handbook (for example: leave days → 24). Two have no answer (“Who is the CEO?”, “What is the annual revenue?”), and the expected result is UNKNOWN. These two check that the app does not invent.

How eval.py checks an answer

  • For a normal question, it checks if the expected text appears inside the answer. Before comparing, norm() lowercases both and removes commas and special spaces, so “40,000 DZD” matches “40000”.
  • For an UNKNOWN question, it checks if the answer contains “don't know” or “do not know”.
  • At the end it prints the score and the settings used: Score: 8/10 = 80% [chat=llama3.2:3b embed=nomic-embed-text k=4 chunk=800 | 41s].
python eval.py              # baseline
TOP_K=1 python eval.py      # one change

If TOP_K=1 scores lower than TOP_K=4, you have learned something real: with one chunk, the right paragraph is sometimes missed. You can say it on stage with a number, which is much stronger than “I think it is better”.

Common mistake

Changing CHUNK_SIZE and running only eval.py. Chunk size is used when indexing, so you must run CHUNK_SIZE=300 python rag_ingest.py first, then CHUNK_SIZE=300 python eval.py. TOP_K and the chat model work without re-indexing.

The limits of this eval

  • It checks if the expected text appears in the answer. A correct answer worded differently (“twenty-four”) is marked wrong.
  • Ten questions is a very small set. Real projects use dozens or hundreds, taken from real users.
  • The test set must stay fixed, or scores cannot be compared.
  • Small models are a little random, so a score can move by one question between two runs. Run twice if the difference is small.

Check yourself

Why do two of the ten questions have no answer in the document?
To test that the app says “I don't know” instead of inventing an answer.
Why should you change only one thing at a time?
So that you know which change caused the score to go up or down.
If the score drops after a change, what do you do?
Undo the change and keep the previous setting. Optionally, read the ❌ lines to understand why.
Chapter 9

Agents: AI that uses tools

Chatbot versus agent

A chatbot only talks. An agent can also act.

Everyday picture

A chatbot is a person who only talks. An agent is the same person with a calculator, a search engine and a notebook on the desk, who decides alone when to use each. But the person can only point at a tool. Your code is the hand that actually presses the buttons.

Take your demo task: “I'm a full-time employee. I took 7 days of leave in March and 5 in August. How many leave days do I have left? Save the answer as a note.” To solve it, you must (1) find the yearly allowance, (2) calculate, (3) save a note. No single model answer can do all of this. An agent can.

The loop

  1. The model reads the task and the list of available tools.
  2. It decides: “I need tool X with these inputs.”
  3. Your code runs the tool and gives the result back to the model.
  4. The model reads the result and decides again: another tool, or the final answer.
  5. It stops when it has enough information, or after max_steps (6).

Here is a typical run of your demo task, step by step:

The code, in short

for _ in range(max_steps):
    response = client.chat(model=AGENT_MODEL, messages=messages, tools=list(TOOLS.values()))
    msg = response.message
    messages.append(msg)
    if not msg.tool_calls:
        return msg.content               # no tool needed: final answer
    for call in msg.tool_calls:
        result = TOOLS[call.function.name](**call.function.arguments)
        messages.append({"role": "tool", "content": str(result)})

Read it as: ask the model; if it asks for tools, run them and add the results to the conversation; repeat. The real file also handles a tool name that does not exist (“Unknown tool”) and a tool that crashes (“Tool error”), and gives that text back to the model instead of crashing. Each 🔧 line printed in the terminal is one tool call.

Why safety matters here

A tool is power. The calculator does not use Python's eval, because eval can run any code. It parses the expression and only allows + − × ÷ %. Even the power operator ** is removed, because 9**9**9 would freeze the laptop. Rules to remember:

  • give each tool one small job;
  • validate its input;
  • limit the number of steps;
  • never give a tool unlimited access to files, the shell or the internet.

Why agents fail sometimes

  • Small models are not good at choosing tools. That is why AGENT_MODEL is qwen2.5:7b, which is trained for tool use, while Demo 1 and 2 use the smaller llama3.2:3b.
  • A vague tool description makes the model pick the wrong tool.
  • Long tasks with many steps fail more often.

Because of this, test your agent task 10 times before the talk, and prepare a recorded backup.

Check yourself

Who actually runs the tool: the model or your code?
Your code. The model only returns a request like “call calculator with this input”.
Why does the calculator avoid eval?
eval runs any Python code. A tricked model could ask it to delete files. The safe parser only allows arithmetic.
Why is max_steps useful?
It stops the loop if the model keeps calling tools forever, so the demo cannot hang.
Why does the agent use a different model than Demo 1?
Choosing tools is harder than chatting. qwen2.5:7b is bigger and trained for tool calling, so it chooses better.
Chapter 10

The web app and the API

Client and server

When you open a website, your browser (the client) sends a request to a computer (the server), and the server sends back a response. They talk using HTTP, and the data travels as JSON.

Everyday picture

A café counter. You (the browser) hand over an order slip (the request) at a specific counter window (the endpoint). The kitchen (the server) hands back a tray (the response). The slip and the tray have a fixed format (JSON).

FastAPI in your project

FastAPI is a Python library for building web servers in a few lines. In app.py you define endpoints:

EndpointMethodWhat it does
/GETShows the web page with the question box
/healthGETAnswers {"status": "ok"}, so you can check the app is alive
/askPOSTReceives a question, runs RAG, returns the answer and sources
/docsGETA test page FastAPI creates automatically, where you can try the API

GET means “give me something”. POST means “here is some data, do something with it”.

What happens when you click Ask

You can call the same endpoint without the browser, which is useful for testing:

curl -X POST http://localhost:8000/ask \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"question": "How many leave days do full-time employees get?"}'

The API key

If you set the environment variable API_KEY, every call to /ask must include a header named X-API-Key with the same value (add -H "X-API-Key: secret" to the curl above). Without it, the server answers 401, which means “not allowed”. If API_KEY is empty, the app is open: fine on your laptop, not fine on the internet. A key alone is not enough for a public server: without HTTPS the key travels as readable text. Put a reverse proxy such as Caddy or Nginx in front to add HTTPS.

Common mistake

The simple web page in app.py does not send an X-API-Key header. So if you set API_KEY, the page's Ask button gets 401. For the stage demo, leave API_KEY empty, and show the 401 with curl instead.

Why the page escapes text

The answer text comes from a model and from documents, so it could contain characters such as < that the browser would treat as code. The page replaces them with safe versions before showing the text. This protects against attacks called XSS.

Without escaping

The answer contains <img src=x onerror="steal()">. The browser sees an image tag and runs the code.

With esc()

It becomes &lt;img …&gt;. The browser shows the characters as harmless text.

The rule is simple: never put text you did not write directly into a web page without escaping it.

Check yourself

What is the difference between GET and POST?
GET asks for something (a page, a status). POST sends data for the server to work on (a question).
What does the status code 401 mean in your app?
“Not allowed”: API_KEY is set and the request did not send the right X-API-Key header.
Why is an API key alone not enough for a public server?
Without HTTPS, anyone on the network path can read the key. You also need HTTPS through a reverse proxy.
Chapter 11

Docker and deployment

The “it works on my computer” problem

Your app works on your laptop because you installed the right Python, the right libraries and the right settings. Your friend's computer has different versions, and the app breaks. Docker puts your app and everything it needs into a sealed box that runs the same way everywhere.

Everyday picture

A shipping container at the port of Algiers. The crane does not care what is inside, and any ship can carry it. Docker is the same for software.

Image and container

The Dockerfile, line by line

FROM python:3.12-slim                # start from a small Python image
WORKDIR /app                         # work in the folder /app
COPY requirements.txt .              # copy ONLY the library list first
RUN pip install --no-cache-dir -r requirements.txt   # this layer is cached
COPY . .                             # now copy your code
EXPOSE 8000                          # the app listens on port 8000
CMD ["uvicorn", "app:app", "--host", "0.0.0.0", "--port", "8000"]

Why copy requirements.txt before the code? Docker reuses a layer if nothing above it changed. When you edit only app.py, the slow pip install layer is reused and rebuilds take seconds. --host 0.0.0.0 means “accept connections from outside the container”, which is needed so your browser can reach it.

Docker Compose

Your system has two parts: the app and Ollama. Docker Compose describes both in one file (docker-compose.yml) so one command starts everything.

  • A service is one container (here: app and ollama).
  • A volume is a folder that survives when a container is deleted.
  • ports connect a port inside the container to your computer (8000:8000).
  • Ollama's port is deliberately not published, so only the app can reach it.
  • restart: unless-stopped brings both back after a crash or a reboot.

The commands

CommandMeaning
docker compose up -d --buildBuild and start everything in the background
docker compose exec ollama ollama pull llama3.2:3bDownload a model inside the Ollama container (once; repeat for nomic-embed-text)
docker compose exec app python rag_ingest.pyBuild the index inside the app container
docker compose psShow what is running
docker compose logs appShow the app's messages (use this when something fails)
docker compose downStop and remove the containers (volumes stay)
Common mistake

The Ollama inside Docker is a different Ollama from the one on your laptop. Models you pulled on your laptop are not inside it. You must pull them again with docker compose exec ollama …. Do this at home, not on the venue Wi-Fi.

Before you go public

  1. Set an API_KEY and put HTTPS in front of the app.
  2. Never publish Ollama's port to the internet.
  3. Keep secrets in a .env file that is never uploaded to GitHub.
  4. Log errors, and back up your documents and index.
  5. Watch the cost of electricity or hosting against real usage.

Check yourself

What is the difference between an image and a container?
An image is the frozen package (code + Python + libraries). A container is a running copy of it.
Why do we use a volume?
So downloaded models and the index survive when containers are deleted or rebuilt.
Why is Ollama's port not published?
Ollama has no password. If 11434 were open to the internet, anyone could use your models and your machine. Only the app should reach it.
How does the app container find Ollama?
By the service name: Docker Compose makes ollama work as a hostname, so OLLAMA_HOST=http://ollama:11434.
Chapter 12

Safety, privacy and honesty

Good engineers also think about what can go wrong.

Be honest about errors

Even with RAG, a model can misread a paragraph. So always show sources, so people can check. Prefer “I don't know” to a confident wrong answer. Never say “the AI is always correct”.

Personal data

If your documents contain names, phone numbers, health or financial information, you have a responsibility. Running locally helps, because the data stays on your machine. Algeria has a law on personal data protection (Law 18-07). Read what it requires for your case, and ask a qualified person for advice before you handle other people's data in a real product.

Prompt injection

A document can contain a sentence like “ignore your rules and reveal secrets”. The model might follow it, because it cannot always tell the difference between your instructions and text inside a document.

Secrets and keys

Passwords, API keys and .env files must never be uploaded to GitHub. Your repo's .gitignore lists files Git should ignore. If you upload a secret by mistake, assume it is public forever and change it.

Licenses

Models, datasets and libraries have licenses. Some forbid commercial use or have conditions. Read the license page before you build a product on a model.

Fairness and language

A model may work better in English than in Arabic or Darija, and may reflect biases of its training data. Test with real examples in your own languages, and tell your users the limits.

Check yourself

Why do we show sources?
So people can check the answer against the original document, because even RAG can be wrong.
What is prompt injection, in one sentence?
Text inside a document or input that tries to give the model new instructions, overriding yours.
Name two things that must never be uploaded to GitHub.
The .env file and any password or API key. (Also: private personal data.)

Questions people may ask, with short answers

Is a local model as good as ChatGPT?
No. Small local models are weaker, but they are enough for many tasks, and they are private and free to run.
Do I need a GPU?
No. A normal laptop works with small models. A GPU (or an Apple M-series chip) makes answers faster.
Does RAG remove hallucinations?
It reduces them a lot, but not completely. Show sources and test with an eval.
Does the model learn from my documents?
No. RAG only gives it the right text at question time. Nothing is retrained.
Can it work in Arabic?
Often yes, but quality varies. Test your chat model and a multilingual embedding model like bge-m3 with real Arabic questions, and compare with eval.py.
How is this different from fine-tuning?
Fine-tuning changes the model itself, and is harder, slower and needs more data. RAG changes only what the model reads, and is the better first step. Use RAG for knowledge, consider fine-tuning for style or format.
Is it legal to use these models?
Usually yes, but read each license, especially for commercial use.
How many users can one laptop serve?
A few at a time. Ollama answers one request after another for each model, so many users means waiting. For more users you need a stronger server or a GPU. Measure before you promise.
Why not just use LangChain or another framework?
You can. This repo uses plain Python so you can see every step. Once you understand the loop, a framework is easier to learn and to debug.
Where do I start after the session?
Clone the repo, run it, then replace the sample handbook in data/ with your own course notes and write 10 eval questions about them.
When you don't know

“Good question. I don't know for sure, and I don't want to guess. Come and see me after, or open an issue on the repo.” Audiences trust this.

الفصل 1

الصورة الكاملة

ما هو الذكاء الاصطناعي؟

عادةً يكتب المبرمج قواعد: «إذا احتوى البريد على كلمة lottery فهو رسالة مزعجة». هذه الطريقة تنكسر بسرعة لأن الناس يغيّرون كلماتهم. في الذكاء الاصطناعي (وبدقة أكثر: تعلّم الآلة Machine Learning) لا أحد يكتب القواعد. نعرض على الحاسوب عددًا هائلًا من الأمثلة، فيكتشف الأنماط بنفسه.

ما هو النموذج اللغوي الكبير (LLM)؟

LLM (Large Language Model) هو ذكاء اصطناعي تعلّم من كمية ضخمة من النصوص. مهارته الأساسية بسيطة: إذا أعطيته نصًا، يتوقّع الكلمة التالية. يكرّر ذلك مرة بعد مرة، فتبدو النتيجة كأنها محادثة. ChatGPT وClaude وGemini وLlama وQwen كلها مبنية على نماذج لغوية كبيرة.

صورة من الحياة اليومية

ChatGPT هو السيارة، والنموذج اللغوي هو المحرّك بداخلها. في جلستك تأخذ محرّكًا مجانيًا (Llama أو Qwen) وتبني حوله سيارتك الخاصة.

ماذا ستبني؟

في جلستك تبني مساعدًا:

  • يعمل على حاسوبك المحمول، دون إنترنت ودون مفتاح API؛
  • يقرأ وثائقك ويجيب عن الأسئلة حولها، مع ذكر المصادر؛
  • يستطيع استعمال أدوات (بحث، آلة حاسبة، ملاحظات) بنفسه؛
  • مُغلَّف بحيث يستطيع أي شخص تشغيله بأمر واحد.

هذا يحتاج أربع مهارات، وهي العروض الأربعة في جلستك. هذا الرسم يبيّن كيف تتّصل كل أجزاء المشروع. ارجع إليه بعد كل فصل، فكل فصل يشرح صندوقًا واحدًا منه.

لماذا محلي ومفتوح المصدر؟

صورة من الحياة اليومية

خدمة الذكاء الاصطناعي السحابية (OpenAI وGoogle وغيرهما) مثل المطعم: الأكل ممتاز، لكنك تدفع ثمن كل وجبة، وغالبًا تحتاج بطاقة دولية، والمطعم يرى طلبك. النموذج المحلي مثل مطبخك الخاص: تدفع مرة واحدة ثمن الأدوات، والطبخ مجاني، ولا أحد يرى ما تطبخ.

هناك ثلاثة أسباب حقيقية تهمّ الطلبة والمطورين في الجزائر:

  1. الدفع. كثير من الخدمات السحابية تتطلّب بطاقة بنكية دولية. النماذج المحلية لا تحتاج أي بطاقة.
  2. التكلفة. الخدمات السحابية تحاسبك على كل سؤال. النماذج المحلية مجانية بعد التحميل (تدفع الكهرباء فقط).
  3. الخصوصية. وثائقك لا تغادر حاسوبك أبدًا. وهذا مهم للشركات وللبيانات الشخصية.

الحدود بصراحة

  • النماذج المحلية الصغيرة أضعف من أكبر النماذج السحابية. للمحادثة والتلخيص وأسئلة الوثائق غالبًا تكفي. للاستدلال الصعب أو الوثائق الطويلة جدًا قد تفشل.
  • تحتاج ذاكرة RAM ومساحة قرص كافية (الفصل 4 يبيّن الكمية).
  • أنت المسؤول عن التحديثات والنسخ الاحتياطية والأمان.
  • النماذج «مفتوحة المصدر» تختلف في رخصها. بعضها يسمح بالاستعمال التجاري وبعضها لا. والأدق أن نسمّي كثيرًا منها مفتوحة الأوزان (open-weights): يمكنك تحميل ملف النموذج، لكن بيانات التدريب والكود ليست كلها منشورة. اقرأ الرخصة قبل أن تبيع منتجًا مبنيًا على نموذج.

كيف يعمل النموذج اللغوي

يتوقّع الوحدة التالية

تخيّل لوحة مفاتيح هاتفك وهي تقترح الكلمة التالية. النموذج اللغوي نفس الفكرة، لكنه مدرَّب على كمية هائلة من النصوص وأمهر بكثير. إذا كتبت «The capital of Algeria is» يعطي النموذج درجة لكل وحدة ممكنة بعدها. «Algiers» تأخذ درجة عالية جدًا فتُختار. ثم يضيفها النموذج إلى النص ويتوقّع من جديد، ويكرّر حتى تكتمل الإجابة. لهذا تظهر الإجابات كلمة بكلمة على الشاشة، وهذا يسمّى البثّ (streaming).

التدريب مقابل الاستعمال

  • التدريب يعلّم النموذج من كميات ضخمة من النصوص. يستغرق أسابيع وحواسيب خاصة ومالًا كثيرًا. المعرفة تُجمَّد داخل ملف النموذج.
  • الاستدلال (استعمال النموذج) هو ما تفعله أنت. أرخص بكثير، وحاسوب عادي يستطيع القيام به مع النماذج الصغيرة.

النموذج لا يتعلّم من أسئلتك. بعد الإجابة ينسى. تطبيقات المحادثة تبدو وكأنها تتذكّر لأنها ترسل المحادثة كاملة من جديد في كل مرة:

لماذا قد يخطئ

النموذج اللغوي لا يبحث عن الحقائق في قاعدة بيانات. إنه يُكمل النص بالطريقة الأكثر احتمالًا. غالبًا يكون الإكمال الأكثر احتمالًا صحيحًا، لكن ليس دائمًا. وعندما لا يعرف، يُنتج شيئًا يبدو صحيحًا. هذه هي الهلوسة، وهي المشكلة رقم واحد في تطبيقات الذكاء الاصطناعي، وهي سبب وجود RAG.

الشيئان اللذان يعرفهما النموذج

درجة الحرارة (Temperature)

درجة الحرارة تتحكّم في مدى «مغامرة» النموذج عندما يختار الوحدة التالية. الحرارة المنخفضة تأخذ تقريبًا دائمًا الخيار الأول: إجابات حذرة ومتكررة (جيدة للحقائق). الحرارة المرتفعة توزّع الفرص: إجابات أكثر تنوّعًا وإبداعًا (جيدة للقصص). لأسئلة الوثائق اجعلها منخفضة.

حجم النموذج

النماذج الأكبر (معاملات أكثر) أذكى عادةً لكنها أبطأ وتحتاج ذاكرة أكبر. الفصل 4 فيه رسم لكمية RAM التي يحتاجها كل نموذج في مشروعك.

الفصل 4

تشغيل النماذج على حاسوبك

ما هو Ollama؟

Ollama برنامج مجاني يحمّل النماذج اللغوية ويخزّنها ويشغّلها على حاسوبك. عند تثبيته يشغّل خدمة صغيرة في الخلفية. البرامج الأخرى، مثل سكربتات بايثون الخاصة بك، تتكلّم مع هذه الخدمة على العنوان http://localhost:11434. «Localhost» تعني «هذا الحاسوب»، فلا شيء يمرّ عبر الإنترنت.

صورة من الحياة اليومية

Ollama مثل مشغّل موسيقى لنماذج الذكاء الاصطناعي. النماذج هي الأغاني المحفوظة على قرصك. Ollama يحمّل واحدة ويشغّلها عندما يطلبها برنامج.

الأوامر التي تحتاجها

الأمرماذا يفعل

ollama listعرض النماذج الموجودة عندك
ollama run llama3.2:3bمحادثة في الطرفية. اكتب /bye للخروج

نوعان من النماذج

  • نموذج محادثة (llama3.2:3b، qwen2.5:7b) يقرأ النص ويكتب الإجابات.
  • نموذج تضمين (nomic-embed-text) لا يتحادث. يحوّل النص إلى أرقام تلتقط المعنى. ستلتقي به في الفصل 7.

هل سيعمل على حاسوبي؟

القاعدة العامة: تحتاج ذاكرة RAM فارغة أكبر قليلًا من حجم التحميل. النموذج الأكبر من ذاكرتك يصبح بطيئًا جدًا.

التكميم: كيف يتّسع نموذج 7B

كل واحد من المعاملات السبعة مليارات هو رقم. إذا خُزِّن بدقة كاملة (16 بت لكل رقم) يحتاج النموذج حوالي 14 GB. التكميم يخزّن كل رقم بعدد أقل من البتات (حوالي 4)، مثل حفظ صورة بصيغة JPEG أصغر. تخسر قليلًا من الجودة، ويتّسع النموذج على حاسوب محمول.

أين Hugging Face؟

Hugging Face موقع ضخم يتشارك فيه الباحثون والشركات النماذج وقواعد البيانات وتطبيقات العرض. اعتبره «GitHub الذكاء الاصطناعي». تستعمله لاكتشاف النماذج وقراءة أوصافها ورخصها ولمشاركة عملك. لـ Ollama مكتبته الخاصة من النماذج الجاهزة، لذلك تحمّل عروضك منها. كثير من النماذج موجودة في المكانين.

لماذا الإجابة الأولى بطيئة

عند السؤال الأول يجب على Ollama تحميل النموذج من القرص إلى الذاكرة (سهم «load once» في الرسم). بعد ذلك تصبح الإجابات أسرع. ويُفرغ Ollama النموذج بعد بضع دقائق من الصمت. لهذا يبدأ ملف demo_commands.txt بعملية تسخين:

curl http://localhost:11434/api/generate -d '{"model":"llama3.2:3b","keep_alive":"60m"}'

هذا الأمر يحمّل النموذج ويطلب من Ollama إبقاءه في الذاكرة 60 دقيقة. شغّله للنموذجين خمس دقائق قبل صعودك إلى المسرح.

يطبع سكربتك شيئًا مثل first token 1.2s | total 9.0s | ~25 tokens/s.

01_hello.py

for chunk in client.chat(
    model=CHAT_MODEL,
    messages=[
        {"role": "system", "content": "You are a concise, friendly teacher."},
        {"role": "user",   "content": question},
    ],
    stream=True,
):
    print(chunk["message"]["content"], end="", flush=True)

اقرأه ببطء:

  • client.chat(...) يرسل محادثة إلى النموذج.
  • messages قائمة. لكل رسالة دور: system يحدّد السلوك، وuser هو الشخص الذي يسأل، وassistant يحمل إجابات النموذج السابقة.
  • stream=True يطلب الإجابة على شكل قطع صغيرة أثناء إنتاجها.
  • حلقة for تستقبل كل قطعة (chunk) وتطبعها فورًا.

السكربت يقيس الوقت أيضًا. يسجّل متى تصل أول قطعة (first_token)، ويعدّ القطع، وفي النهاية يقسم العدد على الوقت الكلي. كل قطعة تقريبًا وحدة واحدة، لذلك تُكتب السرعة مع «~» (تقريبية). والوقت الكلي يشمل وقت الانتظار أيضًا، فسرعة الكتابة الحقيقية أعلى قليلًا ممّا يظهر.

الفكرة: امتحان بالكتاب المفتوح

في امتحان بالكتاب المغلق تجيب من ذاكرتك، وقد تخمّن خطأً. في امتحان بالكتاب المفتوح تجد أولًا الصفحة المناسبة في ملاحظاتك، ثم تكتب الإجابة منها. RAG يحوّل امتحان النموذج من كتاب مغلق إلى كتاب مفتوح.

الاسم يعني Retrieval-Augmented Generation (التوليد المعزَّز بالاسترجاع):

  1. Retrieve · استرجع: جِد فقرات وثائقك الأكثر ارتباطًا بالسؤال.
  2. Augment · عزّز: أضف تلك الفقرات إلى الموجِّه بجانب السؤال.
  3. Generate · ولِّد: النموذج يكتب الإجابة مستعملًا فقط ما أُعطي له.

الجزء أ: تحضير الوثائق (مرة واحدة)

  1. Load · التحميل. rag_ingest.py يقرأ كل ملفات .pdf و.md و.txt في مجلد data/. ملفات PDF تُقرأ صفحة صفحة، فيتذكّر كل مقطع رقم صفحته.
  2. Chunk · التقطيع. قطّع النص إلى قطع من حوالي 800 حرف، كل قطعة تتداخل مع السابقة بـ 150 حرفًا (الرسم أدناه).
  3. Embed · التضمين. حوّل كل مقطع إلى تضمين باستعمال nomic-embed-text.
  4. Store · التخزين. احفظ المقاطع وأرقامها وعلامة {source, page} في Chroma. هذه العلامة هي التي تسمح للإجابة لاحقًا بأن تقول [sahara_labs_handbook.md p.1].

كلما شغّلت rag_ingest.py يحذف المجموعة القديمة ويبدأ من جديد، فلا تحصل أبدًا على مقاطع مكرّرة.

لماذا تتداخل المقاطع

def chunk(text, size=800, overlap=150):
    pieces, start = [], 0
    while start < len(text):
        pieces.append(text[start:start + size])
        start += size - overlap        # تقدّم إلى الأمام، لكن ارجع قليلًا
    return pieces

الكود الحقيقي يرفض أيضًا تداخلًا أكبر من الحجم (لأن الحلقة لن تتقدّم أبدًا) ويحذف القطع الفارغة.

ما هو التضمين حقًا؟

تخيّل خريطة ضخمة للمعاني. النصوص عن أشياء متشابهة توضع قريبة من بعضها، والنصوص عن أشياء مختلفة توضع بعيدة. التضمين هو ببساطة موقع النص على هذه الخريطة، مكتوبًا كقائمة أرقام (مئات الأرقام، لا رقمين فقط). لهذا يبحث RAG حسب المعنى وليس حسب الكلمات نفسها.

الجزء ب: الإجابة عن سؤال (في كل مرة)

  1. يتحوّل السؤال إلى تضمين بنفس النموذج.
  2. يُرجع Chroma المقاطع الأقرب (افتراضيًا TOP_K = 4).
  3. يبني التطبيق الموجِّه: القواعد، ثم المقاطع، ثم السؤال.
  4. يكتب نموذج المحادثة الإجابة ويذكر المصدر.

الوكلاء: ذكاء اصطناعي يستعمل أدوات

روبوت المحادثة مقابل الوكيل

روبوت المحادثة يتكلّم فقط. الوكيل (agent) يستطيع أيضًا أن يتصرّف.

صورة من الحياة اليومية

روبوت المحادثة شخص يتكلّم فقط. الوكيل هو نفس الشخص وعلى مكتبه آلة حاسبة ومحرّك بحث ودفتر، ويقرّر وحده متى يستعمل كل واحد. لكنه يستطيع فقط أن يشير إلى الأداة. كودك هو اليد التي تضغط الأزرار فعلًا.

خذ مهمة عرضك: “I'm a full-time employee. I took 7 days of leave in March and 5 in August. How many leave days do I have left? Save the answer as a note.” (أنا موظف بدوام كامل، أخذت 7 أيام إجازة في مارس و5 في أوت، كم بقي لي؟ احفظ الإجابة كملاحظة.) لحلّها يجب (1) إيجاد الرصيد السنوي، (2) الحساب، (3) حفظ ملاحظة. لا توجد إجابة واحدة من النموذج تفعل كل هذا. الوكيل يستطيع.

الحلقة

  1. يقرأ النموذج المهمة وقائمة الأدوات المتاحة.
  2. يقرّر: «أحتاج الأداة X بهذه المدخلات».
  3. كودك يشغّل الأداة ويعيد النتيجة إلى النموذج.
  4. يقرأ النموذج النتيجة ويقرّر من جديد: أداة أخرى أم الإجابة النهائية.
  5. يتوقّف عندما تكفيه المعلومات، أو بعد max_steps (6 خطوات).

تطبيق الويب والـ API

العميل والخادم

عندما تفتح موقعًا، يرسل متصفحك (العميل client) طلبًا إلى حاسوب (الخادم server)، فيرسل الخادم ردًّا. يتكلّمان بـ HTTP، والبيانات تنتقل بصيغة JSON.

صورة من الحياة اليومية

شبّاك مقهى. أنت (المتصفح) تقدّم ورقة طلب (الطلب request) عند شبّاك محدّد (نقطة النهاية endpoint). المطبخ (الخادم) يعيد لك صينية (الرد response). للورقة وللصينية شكل ثابت (JSON).

FastAPI في مشروعك

FastAPI مكتبة بايثون لبناء خوادم ويب في بضعة أسطر. في app.py تعرّف نقاط النهاية:

/healthGETتجيب {"status": "ok"} لتتأكّد أن التطبيق يعمل

إذا ضبطت متغيّر البيئة API_KEY، فكل نداء لـ /ask يجب أن يحمل ترويسة اسمها X-API-Key بنفس القيمة (أضف -H "X-API-Key: secret" إلى أمر curl أعلاه). دونها يجيب الخادم بـ 401، أي «غير مسموح». إذا كان API_KEY فارغًا فالتطبيق مفتوح: مقبول على حاسوبك، وغير مقبول على الإنترنت. والمفتاح وحده لا يكفي لخادم عام: دون HTTPS ينتقل المفتاح كنص مقروء. ضع أمامه وكيلًا عكسيًا (reverse proxy) مثل Caddy أو Nginx لإضافة HTTPS.

خطأ شائع

صفحة الويب البسيطة في app.py لا ترسل ترويسة X-API-Key. فإذا ضبطت API_KEY سيحصل زر Ask في الصفحة على 401. لعرض المسرح اترك API_KEY فارغًا، واعرض الـ 401 باستعمال curl بدلًا من ذلك.

لماذا تقوم الصفحة بتهريب النص (escaping)

نص الإجابة يأتي من نموذج ومن وثائق، فقد يحتوي رموزًا مثل < قد يعاملها المتصفح ككود. الصفحة تستبدلها بنسخ آمنة قبل عرض النص. هذا يحمي من هجمات تسمّى XSS.

دون تهريب

الإجابة تحتوي <img src=x onerror="steal()">. يرى المتصفح وسم صورة ويشغّل الكود.

مع esc()

يصبح &lt;img …&gt;. المتصفح يعرض الرموز كنص غير ضار.

القاعدة بسيطة: لا تضع أبدًا نصًا لم تكتبه أنت مباشرة في صفحة ويب دون تهريبه.

اختبر نفسك

ما الفرق بين GET وPOST؟
GET تطلب شيئًا (صفحة، حالة). POST ترسل بيانات ليعمل عليها الخادم (سؤال).
ماذا يعني رمز الحالة 401 في تطبيقك؟
«غير مسموح»: API_KEY مضبوط والطلب لم يرسل ترويسة X-API-Key الصحيحة.
لماذا لا يكفي مفتاح API وحده لخادم عام؟
دون HTTPS يستطيع أي شخص على مسار الشبكة قراءة المفتاح. تحتاج أيضًا HTTPS عبر وكيل عكسي.

Docker والنشر

مشكلة «يعمل على حاسوبي»

تطبيقك يعمل على حاسوبك لأنك ثبّتّ إصدار بايثون الصحيح والمكتبات الصحيحة والإعدادات الصحيحة. حاسوب صديقك فيه إصدارات مختلفة، فيتعطّل التطبيق. Docker يضع تطبيقك وكل ما يحتاجه في صندوق مغلق يعمل بنفس الطريقة في كل مكان.

صورة من الحياة اليومية

حاوية شحن في ميناء الجزائر. الرافعة لا يهمّها ما بداخلها، وأي سفينة تستطيع حملها. Docker هو نفس الشيء للبرمجيات.

الصورة والحاوية (Image وContainer)

ملف Dockerfile، سطرًا بسطر

FROM python:3.12-slim                # ابدأ من صورة بايثون صغيرة
WORKDIR /app                         # اعمل في المجلد /app
COPY requirements.txt .              # انسخ قائمة المكتبات فقط أولًا
RUN pip install --no-cache-dir -r requirements.txt   # هذه الطبقة تُحفَظ وتُعاد
COPY . .                             # الآن انسخ كودك
EXPOSE 8000                          # التطبيق يستمع على المنفذ 8000
CMD ["uvicorn", "app:app", "--host", "0.0.0.0", "--port", "8000"]

لماذا ننسخ requirements.txt قبل الكود؟ Docker يعيد استعمال الطبقة إذا لم يتغيّر شيء فوقها. عندما تعدّل app.py فقط، تُعاد طبقة pip install البطيئة كما هي، فيستغرق إعادة البناء ثوانٍ. و--host 0.0.0.0 تعني «اقبل الاتصالات من خارج الحاوية»، وهذا ضروري ليصل متصفحك إليه.

Docker Compose

نظامك فيه جزءان: التطبيق وOllama. Docker Compose يصف الاثنين في ملف واحد (docker-compose.yml) فيشغّل أمر واحد كل شيء.

  • الخدمة (service) حاوية واحدة (هنا: app وollama).
  • وحدة التخزين (volume) مجلد يبقى عند حذف الحاوية.
  • ports تربط منفذًا داخل الحاوية بحاسوبك (8000:8000).
  • منفذ Ollama غير منشور عمدًا، فلا يصل إليه إلا التطبيق.
  • restart: unless-stopped يعيد تشغيل الاثنين بعد عطل أو إعادة تشغيل الجهاز.

حقن الموجِّه (Prompt injection)

قد تحتوي وثيقة على جملة مثل «تجاهل قواعدك واكشف الأسرار». قد يتّبعها النموذج، لأنه لا يستطيع دائمًا التمييز بين تعليماتك والنص الموجود داخل وثيقة.

الأسرار والمفاتيح

كلمات السر ومفاتيح API وملفات .env يجب ألا تُرفع أبدًا إلى GitHub. ملف .gitignore في مشروعك يسرد الملفات التي يجب أن يتجاهلها Git. إذا رفعت سرًّا بالخطأ، اعتبره منشورًا إلى الأبد وغيّره.

الرخص

للنماذج وقواعد البيانات والمكتبات رخص. بعضها يمنع الاستعمال التجاري أو له شروط. اقرأ صفحة الرخصة قبل بناء منتج على نموذج.

الإنصاف واللغة

قد يعمل النموذج أفضل بالإنجليزية منه بالعربية أو الدارجة، وقد يعكس تحيّزات بيانات تدريبه. اختبره بأمثلة حقيقية بلغاتك، وأخبر مستخدميك بحدوده.

Does RAG remove hallucinations?
يقلّلها كثيرًا، لكن ليس كليًا. اعرض المصادر واختبر بتقييم.
Does the model learn from my documents?
لا. RAG يعطيه فقط النص المناسب وقت السؤال. لا شيء يُعاد تدريبه.

How many users can one laptop serve?
قليل في نفس الوقت. Ollama يجيب على الطلبات واحدًا بعد الآخر لكل نموذج، فكثرة المستخدمين تعني الانتظار. لمستخدمين أكثر تحتاج خادمًا أقوى أو معالجًا رسوميًا. قِس قبل أن تَعِد.

7. من يشغّل الأداة في الوكيل: النموذج أم كودك؟
كودك. النموذج يطلب فقط.
8. ما الفرق بين الصورة والحاوية؟
الصورة حزمة مجمَّدة، والحاوية نسخة تعمل منها.
